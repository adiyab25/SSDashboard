## Summary

This feature connects each of a brand's Shopify stores to the app, so orders, products, and customers flow in on their own and the ledger's stock flows back out. Each store is joined through a small app the brand creates in its own Shopify organization, set up on a call with the vendor's onboarding person while the owner types the secret into the app (owner decisions Q-13.1 and Q-13.8, recommended). A webhook (a message Shopify sends when something changes) only tells the app *what* changed; the app then reads the current record back and applies it through one mapping path, the same path a sweep every 5 minutes and the first backfill use, so a lost webhook shows up within 15 minutes, and an event that fails to apply is listed, raises a task, and is retried by the sweep. Stock is owned by the ledger: the first push waits for the stock ledger's online gate and a confirmed preview, Shopify's sellable quantity is set with a compare and set, a higher Shopify figure is pushed down at once and a lower one waits for a person (Q-13.6). Every write to Shopify is a recorded command with a visible state, and a failed one raises an event that opens a task.

## Requirements

**User stories**:
- As the brand's manager, I want to connect each Shopify store once, on a call with the vendor, and be told plainly when the app sits in the wrong Shopify organization or lacks customer data access, so that I never wonder if orders are arriving.
- As a support agent, I want a new Shopify order in the app within seconds, never more than 15 minutes late, and an order that fails to arrive shown to someone, so that the confirmation queue is always complete and oldest first.
- As an operations agent, I want an edit in Shopify (a size swap, a removed piece) to reach the app as an edit, never as a return, and an exchange made by editing a delivered order to become a linked replacement order, so that the return rate stays true and the replacement piece is held.
- As the warehouse, I want Shopify to sell only what the ledger says we hold online, and a change made in Shopify's stock screen to be put right at once when it would oversell and shown to a person when it undersells, so that we neither oversell nor lose track.
- As the developer of the confirmation queue (18) and of returns write back (37), I want one command shape and one failure path for every write to Shopify, so that my feature never calls Shopify itself.
- As the vendor, I want every Shopify behaviour proven on recorded fixtures with no live call, so that CI never depends on a real store or touches customer data.

**Acceptance criteria**:

*Connecting a store*
- **AC-1** (connect): a manager (`settings.manage`) connects a store on `POST /api/connections/shopify` with a label, a store code, the shop domain (`<name>.myshopify.com`), the app's client id and client secret, and who owns the app (the brand's organization, or a person's account). In one transaction the channels module creates the store (kind `shopify`) and its own channel (kind `shopify`, default store this store, one channel per store), the connectors module creates the connection bound to that store with health `connecting`, the credentials encrypted, and the audit entry `connection.created`, and a `connect` job is enqueued. The job obtains a token by the client credentials grant, reads the shop (name, currency, time zone), the granted scopes, the locations, and one recent order, registers the webhook subscriptions, fills the store's names and public names from the shop name (editable after), and sets health `ok`, or `failing` with one code: `APP_NOT_INSTALLED` (the screen names both causes: not installed on this store, or the app and the store are in different Shopify organizations), `APP_ORGANIZATION_MISMATCH` (Shopify's `shop_not_permitted`), `CREDENTIALS_REJECTED`, `SCOPES_MISSING` (with the missing handles), `PROTECTED_DATA_MISSING` (the recent order comes back without shipping name, phone, or address), `CURRENCY_MISMATCH` (not the install's `currency` setting), or `PUBLIC_URL_NOT_HTTPS`. Warnings that keep health `ok` but show on the connection: `SHOP_TIMEZONE_DIFFERS` (the shop's time zone is not the install's `timezone`) and `APP_OWNED_BY_PERSON`. A second connection to the same shop domain answers 409 `SHOP_ALREADY_CONNECTED`; a store code in use 409 `STORE_CODE_TAKEN`.
- **AC-2** (secrets): the client id and secret are stored only as AES-256-GCM ciphertext under `CONNECTOR_SECRET_KEY`, with the key version beside it. No response, log line, audit entry, error, inbox row, or command payload carries the secret or an access token; the screen shows "set" and the last 4 characters of the client id. Access tokens live only in the worker's memory and are fetched again 60 seconds before they expire. Replacing the keys (`PUT /api/connections/:id/credentials`, reason required) writes `connection.credentials_replaced` without values and runs the `connect` checks again. The connect form is typed into by the owner; the runbook forbids sending keys by message (Q-13.8).
- **AC-3** (scopes over time): the required scope list holds the slice 1a scopes plus the slice 1b scopes for refunds and returns write back as confirmed in milestone 1, so the owner approves one app version per store; the hourly check compares the granted scopes with the list of the running release and sets health `degraded` with `SCOPES_MISSING` and the handles when a release needs more, which the screen explains as "approve the new app version in Shopify".

*Webhooks and the inbox*
- **AC-4** (signature first): `POST /api/hooks/shopify/:connectionId` reads at most 2 MB of raw body (413 above), and before parsing anything checks `X-Shopify-Hmac-SHA256` against a base64 HMAC SHA256 of the raw bytes keyed with that connection's client secret, compared in constant time, and checks `X-Shopify-Shop-Domain` equals the connection's shop domain. A missing or wrong signature, a wrong shop, an unknown connection id, or an archived connection all answer the same 401 with no body detail, write no inbox row, and count a failure on the connection (at most one write per connection per minute).
- **AC-5** (durable inbox): a verified delivery inserts one `inbound_events` row (dedupe key `X-Shopify-Webhook-Id`, `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING` on (`tenant_id`, `connection_id`, `dedupe_key`)) and enqueues its job in the same transaction, then answers 200; a repeat delivery answers 200 and adds nothing. A paused connection still records events and processes them after it is resumed. Hook routes never answer 404 for module state: the handler asks `LicenseService.acceptsHook` (spec 0009 AC-18) and records `ignored_unlicensed` when told to.
- **AC-6** (no personal data in the inbox): an inbox row's `payload` holds only the topic, the Shopify object id, its update time, and for inventory messages the item, location, and quantity; never a customer name, phone, email, address, note, or line text, from a webhook, a sweep, or a backfill. Processed, ignored, and echo rows are purged after 90 days, failed rows after 180 days, sync runs after 90 days.

*Reading back and applying*
- **AC-7** (one mapper): every order, product, and customer event is processed by reading the current object from the Admin GraphQL API (version pinned at `2026-10`) and applying it through one mapper, whatever brought it (webhook, sweep, backfill), inside one `OrderTransactions.run` unit of work (spec 0013 AC-22) when it touches an order: the read back happens before the unit, the unit writes the connector's own rows first, then calls orders (which locks the order row, then inventory), touches one order, keeps no state between runs, and calls no provider, so a deadlock rerun is safe. A snapshot whose `updatedAt` is not newer than the version last applied is recorded `ignored` with outcome `stale`; a read back that returns nothing (an order outside the 60 days the app can see, a deleted object) is `ignored` with outcome `not_visible`; one read back marks every other `received` event of the same object as `processed` with outcome `coalesced` in the same transaction; the domain change and the event status commit together, so a crash leaves the event `received` and a retry is safe.
- **AC-8** (failed reads are seen and retried, review X1): a transient read error (network, timeout, HTTP 5xx or 429, `THROTTLED`) never ends an event in `failed`: the job retries with backoff and the event stays `received` with its attempts counted; three hours of transient failure on a connection set health `degraded` with `READS_FAILING`. A domain error the mapper cannot handle sets the event `failed` with its code and publishes `InboundEventFailed` (`connectionId`, `subjectType`, `subjectExternalId`, `failureCode`) in the same transaction, which feature 17 turns into one task per order for operations. The connection screen lists failed events on a "Failed reads" tab with retry; the freshness answer carries `failedOrderEvents` and the shell line turns to a warning while it is above 0. The sweep enqueues again any order whose latest event is `failed` (dedupe `sweep-retry:<gid>:<updatedAt>:<attempt>`, at most 3 times a day per order).
- **AC-9** (orders in): a Shopify order that is not a test order becomes one order through `OrderIntake.createFromChannel` (spec 0013 AC-14) in the connection's store and channel, with `channelOrderRef` the Shopify order id, `orderNumber` Shopify's order name, `placedAt` its creation time, lines keyed by the Shopify line item id with `quantity` its current quantity, the Shopify customer passed as `channelCustomer` (`shopify_customer`, the connection id, the customer id), and every other field from *Value sourcing*. The same order name in two stores gives two orders, each shown with its store. Before mapping an order whose line names a Shopify variant with no mapping, the same job reads and applies that product first (review M8); a variant that still cannot map stays unmapped, and when its product maps later the connector links the line with `OrderIntake.linkChannelLineVariant(tx, orderId, channelLineId, variantId)` (spec 0013 AC-25), which reserves it and never counts as an edit.
- **AC-10** (gifted): an order whose total (lines and shipping) is 0 and which carries a tag listed in the setting `shopify_gift_tags` arrives as `gifted` with that tag's reason code; a listed tag on an order with a total above 0 arrives as a `sale` and is listed on the connection screen as "gift tag on a paid order" (owner decision Q-13.3, recommended).
- **AC-11** (edits, and netting for metrics, carried m3 and review M2): before the first Shopify fulfillment, a later change to the order's lines or ship to fields reaches the order through `OrderIntake.applyChannelEdit` with a snapshot of every line item whose current quantity is above 0, at that quantity, so a size change (one M removed, one L added) arrives as an edit removal plus an added line, never as a return. Netting never decides which lines exist: the adapter classifies the order's sales history, pairing a sale with action type `UPDATE` (a removal) with a sale with action type `ORDER` (an addition) of any agreement, on the same variant, the same operating day of `happenedAt` (by `ssd_operating_day`, on purpose, so the operating day cutoff applies), and the same absolute amount, the n-th removal with the n-th addition by sale id, and publishes `ChannelEditNetted` once per pair with both line ids, so the metric dictionary (feature 24) counts neither half. For a recorded fixture day, units and net sales from the app's lines minus the netted pairs equal the sum of that day's product sales in Shopify's own sales history (what Shopify Analytics adds up). Shopify refunds are recorded as `ChannelRefundRecorded`.
- **AC-12** (exchanges made by editing a delivered order, owner decision Q-13.5, recommended): after the first Shopify fulfillment, line items added to the order become one linked replacement order through `createFromChannel` with `relatedOrder: { orderId, relation: 'replacement_for' }` (spec 0013 AC-23), in the same store and channel, numbered `<Shopify order name>-R<n>`, holding only the added lines, so the piece is reserved and the warehouse sees it; the refunded original line arrives as `ChannelRefundRecorded`. Added lines and refunded lines are not sent to `applyChannelEdit`, so no `channel_edit_not_applied` is raised for them. While spec 0013 refuses the link because the original is not yet `delivered`, `partially_delivered`, or `refused` (`RELATED_ORDER_STATE`), the lines wait on the order's mapping and are tried again on every read back and by the hourly check.
- **AC-13** (cancels and fulfillment): a Shopify cancellation calls `OrderIntake.cancelFromChannel` with the reason code `channel.` plus Shopify's cancel reason in lower case (`channel.other` when it does not fit the reason code shape); the first Shopify fulfillment of an order calls `OrderTransitions.recordCarrierDelivery` with `source: { type: 'shopify_fulfillment', id }` and its time, because Shopify itself refuses edits to fulfilled lines.
- **AC-14** (one owner per field): each field has exactly one owner as the *Field ownership* table says; inbound processing never writes an app owned or ledger owned field; when `applyChannelEdit` answers `write_back_open` the event records outcome `held_write_back_open` and changes nothing, and when that command syncs or is resolved one read back of the order is enqueued.
- **AC-15** (customers): `customers/create` and `customers/update` read the customer back, parse its phone with `EgyptianPhone.parse` at this edge, and call `CustomersService.findOrCreate(tx, { phone, channelCustomer, name, seenAt })` (spec 0014 AC-1); outcome `no_phone` writes nothing and is recorded on the event. Orders never call `findOrCreate` from this module: `createFromChannel` does it from `channelCustomer` (spec 0013 value sourcing).
- **AC-16** (products): `products/create` and `products/update` read the product back (variants 250 at a time, up to Shopify's limit of 2,048 per product) and call `CatalogChannelSync.applyProduct` (spec 0012 AC-14), one Shopify product to one style (spec 0012 Q-12.4), with the ids from `connection_external_ids`, then store the returned style and variant ids and each variant's inventory item id. Outcomes `needs_sku`, `invalid_sku`, `sku_mismatch`, `barcode_conflict`, and `option_conflict` are listed on the connection screen and each publishes `ChannelProductNeedsAttention` once per variant and outcome, which feature 17 turns into a task (spec 0012 Q-12.2). `products/delete` marks the mappings removed and changes nothing in the catalog; archiving a connection calls `CatalogChannelSync.unlink` for the styles and variants no other active connection maps.

*Backfill, go live, sweep, freshness*
- **AC-17** (go live and the first backfill, owner decisions Q-13.2 and Q-13.7, recommended): before the orders backfill, a gateway scan reads the distinct payment gateway names of the last 60 days, and the orders backfill refuses to start with 409 `GATEWAYS_UNMAPPED` until each name has a payment method. A manager starts the backfill with a go live instant (default: the instant of the online location's verified opening count, feature 15). It reads all products, then every order Shopify lets the app see (the last 60 days), through bulk operations streamed line by line and never stored. Open orders (not cancelled, not fulfilled) placed before the go live instant enter through `createFromChannel` with a `system_job` actor and `firstStates: { fulfillment: 'confirmed', reasonCode: 'go_live_import' }` (spec 0013 AC-25); open orders placed after it enter as live `new` orders; closed ones enter with the `import` actor and their found first states (`cancelled`, or `shipped` with the carrier delivery time of their first fulfillment). Progress shows on the connection screen; a backfill that stops resumes from its last recorded position.
- **AC-18** (sweep and freshness, carried M3): every 5 minutes each active connection sweeps orders updated since its watermark minus 10 minutes, and every 30 minutes products; a found object newer than its applied version enters the inbox like a webhook. An order changed in Shopify whose webhook never arrives is applied within 15 minutes of the change. Each connection records its last inbound event, last order, and last successful sweep. The back office shell shows a freshness line to every hat with `orders.view`, using the store's public name in the user's language: "Shopify Essentials: last order 3 minutes ago, checked 2 minutes ago", plus "2 Shopify writes failed" and "1 order failed to arrive" when those counts are above 0; it turns to a warning when the last successful orders sweep is older than 15 minutes, health is `failing`, or a count is above 0, and the warning says to tell the manager. An hourly check registers again any webhook subscription Shopify removed.

*Stock*
- **AC-19** (stock push, and the first push): with `step.shopify_stock_push` runnable (Warehouse licensed and the step on), an online location live (it went online only through spec 0011's `OnlineStockGate`, where this feature's checks ran), and the connection's first push confirmed, each connection sets Shopify's `available` quantity at its one mapped stock location to `pushable` (spec 0011 AC-2, `max(onHand − reserved − unfilledDemand, 0)`) minus the setting `shopify_stock_safety_buffer`, floored at 0, for every changed variant whose target differs from what was last pushed, through `inventorySetQuantities` with `changeFromQuantity` the last quantity known in Shopify and an idempotency key, at most 250 quantities per call, each call one outbound command (`shopify.inventory_set`, linked to the connection) holding the values sent. A push runs every minute from `OnlineStockService.changedSince`, and at once (within seconds) after the inbox applies an order whose variants are mapped in another active connection (review M7) and after spec 0011 publishes `ReservationFilled`, `ReservationShortened`, or `StockOverdrawn`. The first push of a connection waits until a preview run shows the full first push ("N variants will change in Shopify", with Shopify's and the ledger's figures) and a manager confirms that count with a reason. Levels whose mapping is removed or whose item is untracked are left out; a per item error drops that item, marks its level with the code (listed on the Products tab), and resends the rest once. With the step off or Warehouse not licensed nothing is pushed and the stock fixture test fails on any write request.
- **AC-20** (stale compare): when Shopify answers `CHANGE_FROM_QUANTITY_STALE`, the job reads the current quantity, waits until the connection has no `received` order event older than the answer, computes the target again, and pushes once more; it never overwrites a Shopify sale the ledger has not yet seen.
- **AC-21** (echo, differences, and which side wins, carried m2, owner decision Q-13.6, recommended): an inbound inventory message never writes a stock movement. A message older than the level's `shopify_seen_at` is `ignored` with outcome `stale`; otherwise it sets the level's `shopify_quantity` and `shopify_seen_at`, the base of the next compare and set. One whose quantity equals the last pushed quantity within 10 minutes of the push is marked `echo`. Any other is checked again 5 minutes later: the job re-reads the level from Shopify, waits until the order inbox has no older `received` event, and compares with the target from `OnlineStockService.forVariants`. Equal records nothing. **Shopify higher than the target** (an oversell risk) records a difference and pushes the target in the next minute. **Shopify lower than the target** (a damaged piece, an exchange line) records a difference and holds that level's push until a person with `stock.receive` acknowledges it with a reason code (`stock_difference.acknowledged`, audited) or the target falls to Shopify's figure. Each recorded difference publishes `StockDriftRecorded`. A nightly reconcile reads every level and applies the same rule.
- **AC-22** (other Shopify locations, review M9 and spec 0011 review M2): the connect job and the nightly reconcile list every other location of the store that holds a nonzero quantity or fulfils online orders. Any such location sets health `degraded` with `STOCK_AT_UNMAPPED_LOCATION` and the count, shows on the Overview tab with the stock it holds, and fails this feature's `OnlineStockGate` check, so no first push happens until the brand turns off online fulfilment there or sets its stock to 0.

*Outbound commands*
- **AC-23** (one door out): other modules write to Shopify only through `ConnectorsService.enqueue(tx, command)` in their own transaction; the kinds and their payload schemas live in `packages/contracts/src/connectors/` (`shopify.inventory_set` built here; `shopify.order_edit` and `shopify.order_cancel` shaped here for feature 18; `shopify.return_record` and `shopify.refund` reserved for features 37 and 35). Order kind payloads hold ids only, never an address or phone. The connectors module turns `OrderEdited` and `OrderCancelled` with `origin = 'person'` into one `shopify.order_edit` or `shopify.order_cancel` command in the publisher's transaction and ignores other origins.
- **AC-24** (failure contract, carried B2): commands run one at a time per connection; a transient error retries with backoff up to 5 attempts; when retries are exhausted or Shopify rejects the command, it becomes `failed` with a provider error code (never provider text) and `OutboundCommandFailed` is published in the same transaction; success makes it `synced` with `OutboundCommandSynced`; `OutboundCommandQueued` and `OutboundCommandResolved` complete the set, each with `commandId`, `connectionId`, `kind`, `linkedType`, `linkedId`. `openCommandsFor(linkedType, id, tx)` returns the `waiting` and `failed` commands; a person retries (`outbound_command.retried`) or resolves as fixed in Shopify with a reason code (`outbound_command.resolved`); a kind whose executing step is off fails its waiting commands with `STEP_OFF` (spec 0009 AC-21). `writeBackAvailable(orderId, kind)` (kind `shopify.order_edit` or `shopify.order_cancel`, spec 0013) is true only when the order's store connection is active and not `failing`, that kind's step is runnable, and its executor is registered; while it is false spec 0013 refuses in app edits with 409 `EDIT_IN_SHOPIFY` and cancels with 409 `CANCEL_IN_SHOPIFY` (spec 0013 AC-7, AC-10, Q-16.3), so in slice 1a, before feature 18, staff edit and cancel Shopify orders in Shopify and the change syncs back.

*Limits, tests, screen*
- **AC-25** (rate limits hold): every Admin API call goes through one client that keeps each connection's throttle state from `extensions.cost.throttleStatus`, waits before a call whose expected cost exceeds what is available, never sends a query whose requested cost exceeds 1,000 points, retries `THROTTLED` errors and HTTP 429 after the advised wait, runs at most one call per connection at a time, and ends a sweep or backfill chunk after 60 seconds, leaving it `partial` to resume. A response whose `X-Shopify-API-Version` differs from the pinned version sets the health warning `API_VERSION_FELL_FORWARD`.
- **AC-26** (fixtures only): every test runs on recorded fixtures; the api test setup replaces `fetch` with one that answers only recorded requests and fails the test on any other, so no test can reach Shopify; fixtures come from a Shopify development store with invented customers, scrubbed of tokens and secrets; signature tests use known good and tampered bodies; a fixture replay server serves the same recordings to Playwright and local development and refuses to start when `NODE_ENV` is `production`.
- **AC-27** (connection screen): a manager opens `/connections` (list) and `/connections/[id]` (detail) and sees status, health and its reason, warnings, freshness, webhook subscriptions, other Shopify locations holding stock, the stock location and payment gateway mappings, backfill progress, the first push preview, unmapped variants, failed reads, and failed commands, with the actions connect, check again, scan gateways, start backfill, confirm the first push, pause, resume, replace keys, and archive (archive removes the webhook subscriptions first). Other hats get the no access state. Both languages, right to left, no hard coded text, logical CSS, keyboard only, WCAG 2.2 AA, axe clean, one `<h1>`, built to `packages/ui/DESIGN.md`; no secret is ever shown.

## Decision

**Chosen option**: Option 2 in [rationale.md](rationale.md): a brand owned Dev Dashboard app per store with the client credentials grant; webhooks as hints, every object read back through one GraphQL mapper shared with the sweep and the backfill; inbound failures as visible and retried as outbound ones; stock owned by the ledger, gated before its first push, and pushed with compare and set; one outbound command door with a failure contract.

## Feature design

### Where it lives

- `apps/api/src/channels/` (module `channels`, bundle `core`, no toggle): `ChannelsService` (exported; the only writer of `stores` and `channels`), `README.md`. No controller in this feature; stores are created through the connect route.
- `apps/api/src/connectors/` (module `connectors`, bundle `core`, no toggle):
  - `connectors.module.ts`, `connectors.service.ts` (`ConnectorsService`, exported), `connectors.events.ts` (the public event classes), `connectors.errors.ts`.
  - `connections/` (`ConnectionsController`, `ConnectionsService`, `connection-secrets.ts`: AES-256-GCM seal and open), `hooks/` (`ShopifyHooksController`, raw body reader), `inbox/` (`InboxService`, the failed reads list and retry, purge job), `outbox/` (`OutboxService`, the executor registry, `outbound-commands.controller.ts`), `freshness/` (`FreshnessController`).

### Shopify app, auth, and API version

- **App and token** (Q-13.1, Q-13.8): on a call with the vendor's onboarding person, the owner, in the brand's own Shopify organization's Dev Dashboard (with at least two owners on the organization), creates one app per store, selects the scopes below and the protected customer fields (name, phone, address, email), releases a version, installs it on the store, and types the client id and secret into the app's connect form. The runbook checks the organization first, because the client credentials grant works only when the app and the store belong to the same Shopify organization, and forbids sending keys by message. The worker exchanges the client id and secret for an access token at `https://<shop>/admin/oauth/access_token` (`grant_type=client_credentials`, form encoded, no `scope` parameter); the token lasts 86,399 seconds and is cached in the worker's memory per connection and fetched again 60 seconds before expiry. An `app_not_installed` answer maps to `APP_NOT_INSTALLED` (both causes named) and `shop_not_permitted` to `APP_ORGANIZATION_MISMATCH`. A brand that still has a legacy custom app made in the Shopify admin before 2026 may paste its Admin API access token instead (`credentials.kind = 'access_token'`, with that app's API secret as the webhook secret).
- **Scopes** (`SHOPIFY_REQUIRED_SCOPES`, checked against `currentAppInstallation { accessScopes { handle } }` at connect and every hour): `read_orders`, `write_orders`, `write_order_edits`, `read_products`, `read_customers`, `read_inventory`, `write_inventory`, `read_locations`, plus the scopes Shopify's refund and return mutations need for features 35 and 37, confirmed against the development store in milestone 1 and listed in the runbook; if they cannot be confirmed then, the runbook says slice 1b brings a second approval. `read_all_orders` is not requested (Q-13.2).
- **Protected customer data**: checked at connect by reading the most recent order; with no order yet, the first order applied runs the same check and sets `PROTECTED_DATA_MISSING` if needed.
- **Version**: one constant `SHOPIFY_API_VERSION = '2026-10'` (supported until 16 October 2027). Moving to a newer version is a release step with the fixtures recorded again (Follow-up).
- **Webhook subscriptions** (registered per connection with `webhookSubscriptionCreate(topic, webhookSubscription: { uri, format: JSON })`, the shape the reference brand runs on `2026-10`): `ORDERS_CREATE`, `ORDERS_UPDATED`, `ORDERS_EDITED`, `ORDERS_CANCELLED`, `REFUNDS_CREATE`, `PRODUCTS_CREATE`, `PRODUCTS_UPDATE`, `PRODUCTS_DELETE`, `CUSTOMERS_CREATE`, `CUSTOMERS_UPDATE`, `INVENTORY_LEVELS_UPDATE`, `APP_UNINSTALLED`, `BULK_OPERATIONS_FINISH`. The address is `PUBLIC_URL` + `/api/hooks/shopify/<connection id>` and must be https. Subscriptions made through the Admin API are deleted by Shopify after 8 failed deliveries in a row (retried over 4 hours), which is why the hourly check exists. `APP_UNINSTALLED` sets health `failing` with `APP_NOT_INSTALLED`.

### Inbound processing

1. **Hook** (`@Audience('hook')`, `@RequiresModule('connectors')`): a raw body parser on `/api/hooks/*` mounted before the JSON parser in `app.setup.ts`, capped at 2 MB; the client secret is decrypted once and cached in process for 5 minutes per (connection, key version). Signature and shop checks as AC-4, then the inbox insert and the job send in one transaction (`send` with the `db` option, spec 0007 section 1), then 200. `last_inbound_at` is updated at most every 30 seconds per connection, so the connection row is never a hot spot.
2. **Queue**: one pg-boss queue `connector-shopify` for every Shopify job; each job is sent with `group: { id: connectionId }` and the worker uses `groupConcurrency: 1`, so one job per connection runs at a time across all worker processes (the pg-boss v12 group options, `.claude/skills/pg-boss-v12/reference.md`). Priorities: outbound commands 30, inbox events 20, stock push 15, sweeps 10, backfill chunks 0. Every job ends within 60 seconds and sends its own continuation when work remains. A stock push job is sent with a singleton key per connection, so repeats within 10 seconds add none.
3. **Inbox job**: skips unless the event is still `received`; reads the subject back (one GraphQL query for an order, product, or customer); a transient read error throws so pg-boss retries it with backoff (AC-8); compares `updatedAt` with `connection_external_ids.external_version`; applies through the mapper inside `OrderTransactions.run` when an order is touched (the read back is done before the unit; inside it the job reads its own rows again, writes them first, then calls orders; no Shopify call and no state kept between reruns); marks the event and every other `received` event of the same subject (`coalesced`); a domain error the mapper cannot handle sets `failed`, publishes `InboundEventFailed`, and commits only that (the domain writes roll back). Inventory level events use their own payload (no read back) and run the stock rule of AC-21.
4. **Order mapping** (`order-mapper.ts`, the Shopify order shape the reference brand's sync reads on `2026-10`, `src/lib/shopify/orders.ts` in the reference console): see *Value sourcing*. Test orders (`test: true`) are `ignored` with `test_order`. A line whose Shopify variant is unmapped first triggers a product read and apply in the same job (review M8). For an existing order, lines still unmapped in `state.unmappedLines` whose variant now maps are linked with `OrderIntake.linkChannelLineVariant(tx, orderId, channelLineId, variantId)` before `applyChannelEdit` is called, so the link is never seen as a changed variant. After the first fulfillment, added line items go to `exchange-by-edit.ts` (AC-12) and refunded quantities to `ChannelRefundRecorded`; only ship to changes and any other line change go to `applyChannelEdit` (which raises `channel_edit_not_applied` itself).
5. **Edit netting** (`edit-netting.ts`, pure, unit tested; review M2): input is the order's line items and its `agreements` with their `sales`; only sales of line type product count. Removals are sales with action type `UPDATE`; additions are sales with action type `ORDER`, from any agreement, as in the reference brand's rule verified against Shopify Analytics. A removal and an addition pair when they share the variant, the operating day of `happenedAt` (computed by one SQL call to `ssd_operating_day` for the snapshot's distinct instants, never in TypeScript), and the absolute amount; the n-th removal pairs with the n-th addition ordered by sale id. Netting does not change the snapshot. Each new pair (its sale ids not yet in `state.nettedSaleIds`) publishes `ChannelEditNetted` with both line ids. `RETURN` sales stay real reversals: from a refund agreement they publish `ChannelRefundRecorded` once per refund id. The operating day is used on purpose: with the default cutoff of 0 it is the same calendar day the reference brand and Shopify Analytics use, and `SHOP_TIMEZONE_DIFFERS` warns when the shop's day could differ.
6. **Catalog and customers**: as AC-16 and AC-15. A product's `ChannelProductInput` carries the connection's `storeId`, the Shopify product gid as `externalProductId`, the mapped `styleId`, handle, title, product type, vendor, tags, options (Shopify's lone default option sent as none), and per variant its gid, mapped `variantId`, SKU, option values, price, compare at price, barcode, weight in grams, requires shipping, and tracked.

### Backfill, go live, and sweeps

- **Gateway scan** (AC-17, review M6): a bulk read of the last 60 days of orders selecting only ids and payment gateway names fills the Mapping tab; it runs after connect and again on demand. `POST /api/connections/:id/backfill` refuses with 409 `GATEWAYS_UNMAPPED` while a scanned name has no method.
- **Backfill** (AC-17): `bulkOperationRunQuery` for products (variants and inventory item ids nested), then orders filtered on `created_at` from `go_live_at` minus 60 days, with the same selection as the read back. A `BULK_OPERATIONS_FINISH` webhook or a poll every 30 seconds finds the result; the job streams the JSONL from the signed URL (valid one week), groups child lines under their parent by `__parentId`, and feeds each object to the same mapper with an inbox row per object (`source = 'backfill'`, dedupe `backfill:<bulk operation id>:<gid>`). Nothing of the file is stored; the cursor holds the line number reached, so a chunk that stops resumes there. If Shopify refuses the order selection because of nested connection depth (bulk queries allow two nested levels), the orders backfill falls back to paged queries of at most 1,000 points each (Risk).
- **Go live** (Q-13.7, review M4): `go_live_at` is set when the backfill starts. Orders placed before it: open ones enter as `confirmed` (actor `system_job`, reason `go_live_import`), cancelled ones as `cancelled` and fulfilled ones as `shipped` (actor `import`, found states). Orders placed after it are live. The same instant aligns stock, as the contract with feature 15 below states.
- **Sweeps** (AC-18; spec 0007 carried M3): `orders(first: 250, sortKey: UPDATED_AT, query: "updated_at:>='<watermark minus 10 minutes>'")` selecting only `id`, `name`, and `updatedAt`, then an inbox row (`source = 'sweep'`, dedupe `sweep:<gid>:<updatedAt>`) for each one newer than its applied version, and a retry row (`sweep-retry:<gid>:<updatedAt>:<attempt>`, attempt 1 to 3 within the operating day) for each order whose latest event is `failed` (AC-8). The watermark moves to the newest `updatedAt` seen when the run completes; a `partial` run keeps its page cursor. Products the same way every 30 minutes. The 10 minute overlap is the reference brand's safety margin (`getWatermark` in `src/lib/shopify/sync.ts`). **Budget**: a sweep runs every 5 minutes, ends within 60 seconds, and its found events are processed at priority 20, so a lost webhook is applied within 15 minutes; a connection whose last successful orders sweep is older than 15 minutes is `degraded` with `SWEEP_LATE`.
- **Hourly check**: lists `webhookSubscriptions(first: 100)` and creates any missing (topic, address) pair (never deletes another app's subscription); compares granted scopes (AC-3); tries waiting replacement orders again (AC-12).

### Stock push, the gate, echo, and differences

- **Mapping**: the connection's `config.stockLocationId` is the one Shopify location fed from the app's one online location (spec 0011 AC-1). Without it, or with `NoOnlineLocation`, nothing is pushed and health is `degraded` with `NO_STOCK_LOCATION`.
- **Gate checks** (registered with spec 0011's `OnlineStockGate` at module start, each returning `{ key, label, passed, count }`): `shopify.stock_location_mapped` (every active Shopify connection has exactly one stock location; count: connections without one); `shopify.no_other_selling_locations` (no other location of any store holds stock or fulfils online orders, from the latest connect or reconcile run within 24 hours; count: such locations, AC-22); `shopify.first_push_preview` (count: Shopify variants that will change or go to 0, from the latest preview run within one hour, computed against the candidate location through a read spec 0011 is asked to add, below). The gate runs inside an api request, so the checks only read stored runs; they never call Shopify.
- **First push of a connection** (review M4): a `stock_preview` run (bulk read of the mapped location's levels, compared with the targets) stores its counts; the Mapping tab shows "N variants will change in Shopify" with the Shopify and ledger figure of each; `POST /api/connections/:id/stock-push/confirm` with the shown count and a reason sets `stock_push_confirmed_at` (`connection.stock_push_confirmed`, audited), refusing 409 `PREVIEW_STALE` when a newer run counts differently and 409 `GATE_CHECK_FAILED` while this connection fails a gate check. No push runs for a connection without it. A connection added after the location is online goes through the same preview and confirmation.
- **Push** (AC-19): `stock-push` job per connection every minute (cron through `JobRegistry`, capability `connectors.shopify.stock_push`), and at once on an order applied in another connection with shared variants and on spec 0011's `ReservationFilled`, `ReservationShortened`, and `StockOverdrawn`: pages `changedSince({ since: stock_pushed_through })`, computes each target as `max(pushable − shopify_stock_safety_buffer, 0)`, keeps variants mapped to a tracked Shopify inventory item whose target differs from `pushed_quantity` and whose level is not `held_lower`, and sends batches of up to 250 through `inventorySetQuantities` (`name: "available"`, `reason` and `referenceDocumentUri` per the recorded fixture, each quantity with `inventoryItemId`, `locationId`, `quantity`, `changeFromQuantity` = `shopify_quantity`), with the command's idempotency key in the `@idempotent` directive (required from `2026-04`). The batch is written as one `shopify.inventory_set` command before it is sent. On success each level's `pushed_quantity`, `pushed_at`, `push_command_id`, and `shopify_quantity` are set, and `stock_pushed_through` moves to the feed's watermark. A level with `shopify_quantity` unknown is read first. When `changedSince` reports a different online location than before, the job resyncs every level with `OnlineStockService.list`. Per item errors as AC-19.
- **Stale compare** (AC-20): `CHANGE_FROM_QUANTITY_STALE` on any quantity reads those levels, waits for the order inbox to drain, recomputes, and sends a new command; after 3 stale rounds in one run the job ends `partial` and the next minute continues.
- **Echo and differences** (AC-21): an inventory message newer than `shopify_seen_at` updates the level; within 10 minutes of a push and equal to it, `echo`; else a `stock-recheck` job is sent 5 minutes later. Shopify higher: the level is marked for the next push and the difference recorded. Shopify lower: `held_lower` is set and the push skips the level until acknowledged or until the target falls to Shopify's figure, which clears the hold and the difference.
- **Nightly reconcile** (03:30 in the install `timezone`): a bulk read of every location's levels for the store, compared with `OnlineStockService.list` for the mapped location under the same rule, and every other location's nonzero quantities reported (AC-22).

### Contract with feature 15 and spec 0013 at go live (review M4, spec 0011 contract)

- The online location's opening figure is physical on hand counted at one instant, never Shopify's "available" (spec 0011 contract with feature 15); that instant is the backfill's default `go_live_at`.
- Orders placed before that instant that are still open enter as `confirmed` (`go_live_import`) and reserve at creation (spec 0013 Q-16.2), so their pieces, still on the shelf and in the count, are held once.
- Orders placed and fulfilled before that instant are imported as `shipped` and post no shipment movement: their pieces were already gone when the shelf was counted. Spec 0013 is asked to confirm that an import's found `shipped` state enqueues no shipment posting.
- The first push waits for the online gate (spec 0011 AC-19) and each connection's confirmed preview, so nothing is written to Shopify before the count, the orders, and the push agree.

- **Idempotency keys**: `shopify.inventory_set:<connection>:<run id>:<batch>`; `shopify.order_edit:<audit entry id>`; `shopify.order_cancel:<order id>`.
- **Failure contract** (AC-24): transient means a network error, a timeout (30 seconds per request), HTTP 5xx, 429, or `THROTTLED`; rejection means a `userErrors` entry or a 4xx other than 429. `failure_reason` stores `<SHOPIFY CODE or HTTP status>` only. `OutboundCommandFailed` is the event the tasks module (feature 17) listens to; this feature opens no task itself. Retry sets the command back to `waiting` with attempts kept; resolve sets `resolved` with `fixed_in_provider` (here) or `edit_undone` (only through `ConnectorsService.resolve` called by the orders undo of feature 18).

### Rate policy (AC-25)

One `ShopifyClient` per worker process, state per connection: the last `throttleStatus` (`maximumAvailable`, `currentlyAvailable`, `restoreRate`) and each operation's last `actualQueryCost`. Before a call it estimates the cost (last actual, else `requestedQueryCost` recorded in the fixtures) and, if above what is available now (restored at `restoreRate` since the last reading), waits the difference divided by the restore rate. A `THROTTLED` error waits the same way and retries up to 5 times; HTTP 429 waits `Retry-After` or 1 second (Shopify's advised backoff). Page sizes are constants chosen so every query's requested cost stays at or under 900 points; a unit test reads every recorded response and fails above 1,000. Bulk operations are used for the gateway scan, backfill, preview, and reconcile because Shopify does not rate limit their execution. A sweep or backfill chunk also stops at 3,000 points spent.

### State transitions

- `connections.health`: `connecting` to `ok` (connect job passed) or `failing` (a named code); `ok` to `degraded` (`SWEEP_LATE`, `READS_FAILING`, `WEBHOOKS_MISSING`, `SCOPES_MISSING`, `NO_STOCK_LOCATION`, `STOCK_AT_UNMAPPED_LOCATION`, `PROTECTED_DATA_MISSING` found on a later order, `API_VERSION_FELL_FORWARD`) and back when the cause clears; any to `failing` on `APP_NOT_INSTALLED`, `APP_ORGANIZATION_MISMATCH`, or `CREDENTIALS_REJECTED`; `failing` to `ok` only through a passed `connect` check. Warnings (`SHOP_TIMEZONE_DIFFERS`, `APP_OWNED_BY_PERSON`) never change health.
- `connections.status` (spec 0002): `active` to `paused` (no Shopify calls; the inbox still records) and back; `active` or `paused` to `archived` (subscriptions removed first, jobs stop, `CatalogChannelSync.unlink` for what no other active connection maps, mappings kept for history; final).
- `inbound_events.status`: `received` to `processed`, `ignored`, `ignored_unlicensed`, `echo`, or `failed` (domain errors only); `received` stays `received` across transient retries; `failed` to `received` by a retry from the Failed reads tab, and a sweep retry adds a new `received` row for the same order.
- `outbound_commands.sync_state` (spec 0002): `waiting` to `synced` or `failed`; `failed` to `waiting` (retry) or `resolved`; `waiting` to `failed` with `STEP_OFF` or `MODULE_OFF` (spec 0009).
- `connection_stock_levels`: no difference; difference higher (pushed next minute, cleared when equal); difference lower and `held_lower` (cleared by acknowledgement or by the target falling to Shopify's figure).

### Key invariants

- The connectors module writes only `connections`, `inbound_events`, `connection_external_ids`, `connection_stock_levels`, `connection_sync_runs`, and `outbound_commands`; the channels module only `stores` and `channels`. Everything else changes through the named calls of the parallel specs.
- No Shopify call happens outside a worker job of the `connector-shopify` queue; at most one runs per connection.
- An inbox row exists only for a verified delivery or an object the app itself read from Shopify.
- An order event never ends unseen: it is processed, ignored with a reason, or `failed` with an `InboundEventFailed` and a row on the Failed reads tab.
- An order snapshot always holds every Shopify line item with a current quantity above 0; netting never removes a line.
- No inbound path posts a stock movement or changes an app owned or ledger owned field.
- Every stock quantity sent to Shopify is in an `outbound_commands` row before it is sent; nothing is pushed for a connection before its first push is confirmed and the online location passed the gate.
- A Shopify figure lower than the ledger is never raised by a push without a person or the ledger agreeing.
- An order with a `waiting` or `failed` write back command is never changed by a read back.
- A secret is never readable outside `connection-secrets.ts` and the worker's memory.

### Security model

- Personal data (Law 151): order and customer read backs carry names, phones, and addresses; they pass through memory into the orders and customers modules and are never stored in the connectors tables or logs. `subject_label` holds only the Shopify order name. `credentials_ciphertext`, `payload` of order kinds (ids only), and `failure_reason` (codes only) keep the connectors tables free of personal data; the redaction list gains `clientSecret`, `accessToken`, `credentials`, and `shippingAddress` as keys (`packages/config/src/redaction.ts`).
- The hook endpoint is public by necessity and proves itself only by signature and shop domain; it reveals nothing about which connections exist (same 401 for all failures). Rate limiting at the edge is feature 26's Caddy configuration.
- Connecting, mapping, scans, backfill, previews, the first push, failed reads, pause, resume, archive, and keys: manager only (`settings.manage`). Freshness: `orders.view`. Differences: `stock.view` to read, `stock.receive` to acknowledge. Commands: by linked type as the API table says.
- Audit (spec 0010 registry additions, each with English and Arabic labels and its proof test): `connection.created`, `connection.edited`, `connection.credentials_replaced` (reason required), `connection.paused` (reason required), `connection.resumed` (reason required), `connection.archived` (reason required), `connection.backfill_started`, `connection.stock_push_confirmed` (reason required), `inbound_event.retried`, `outbound_command.retried`, `outbound_command.resolved` (reason code), `stock_difference.acknowledged` (reason code). New record types `connection` (reason text, label the store code), `inbound_event` (reason code, label the Shopify order name), and `connection_stock_level` (reason code, label the SKU code). Entries never hold credentials.

## Consequences

**Positive**:
- One mapper for webhooks, sweeps, and backfill means one set of tests proves all three paths, and a lost or out of order webhook can never leave an order half applied.
- An inbound failure is as visible as an outbound one: a task, a list, a count in the shell, and a retry by the sweep.
- The inbox and the connectors tables hold no personal data, so their retention is an audit question, not a Law 151 one.
- Stock is pushed with compare and set and an idempotency key, the first push is previewed and gated, a higher Shopify figure is corrected within a minute, and a lower one is never raised without a person, so the push can neither oversell nor undo a deliberate hold.
- Exchanges made by editing a delivered Shopify order become reserved replacement orders instead of noise.
- The command door gives features 18, 35, and 37 a ready failure path, hold rule, and task event; they only add executors.
- The brand owns the Shopify apps, so its exit package needs no vendor action on Shopify.

**Negative / tradeoffs**:
- Every order event costs one GraphQL read (about a few hundred reads a day per store), and the reads depend on Shopify being up; a Shopify outage delays orders until the sweep catches up.
- Onboarding is a vendor assisted call per brand: one Dev Dashboard app per store, scopes, protected data, gateway mapping, the stock location, the preview; a store built in an agency's organization must be moved or the agency must create the app.
- Two stores sharing the online location can both sell the last piece in the seconds before the second push (Q-13.4); the reference brand's second store sells the first store's SKUs by design, so this window is real.
- A Shopify figure lower than the ledger stays lower until a person acknowledges it, so a mistaken zero in Shopify hides stock until someone looks at the differences page.
- Edit netting relies on Shopify's sales history fields (`agreements`, `sales`, action types), which the reference brand verified on `2026-10` but which Shopify may reshape in a later version; the yearly version move must record the fixtures again.
- Orders older than 60 days are not imported, so a customer's earlier history starts empty unless feature 15 imports it.
- A failed write back and a failed read still need a person; nothing here fixes a rejected edit or an order the rules refuse by itself.
- One read is requested from spec 0011 (candidate location figures for the gate preview) and one confirmation from spec 0013 (no shipment posting for imported `shipped` orders) before milestones 2 and 3.

**Neutral**:
- The baseline gains four tables and two amended ones; whichever of specs 0011 to 0015 lands second regenerates the baseline on top of the first.
- Customers without orders are created from `customers/create` when they have a phone (spec 0014 rule e).
- The freshness line is shown to every hat that sees orders, not only operations and manager (spec 0007 named those two; showing more harms nothing).

## Questions for the owner

Each is decided with the recommended answer and built that way unless the owner says otherwise.

1. **Q-13.1 How does the app reach each Shopify store?** _Decided: the brand creates one app per store in Shopify's Dev Dashboard in its own organization; tokens come from the client credentials grant (recommended)._ Other options: a vendor owned app per store with custom distribution, installed by a link the brand clicks (authorization code grant); or one public app on the Shopify App Store. Why: Shopify stopped new admin custom apps on 1 January 2026, the client credentials grant works only when app and store share an organization, custom distribution still needs one app per store outside a Plus organization, and a public app needs App Store review and protected data review before the pilot; the reference brand already runs exactly this setup on two stores.
2. **Q-13.2 How much order history does the first backfill bring?** _Decided: everything Shopify shows by default, the last 60 days (recommended)._ Other options: only open orders; 12 months or all (needs the `read_all_orders` scope selected on each app). Why: 60 days covers Bosta's 45 to 60 day cash and fee windows for reconciliation and needs no extra scope; deeper history is feature 15's import.
3. **Q-13.3 How does a Shopify order become a gifted order?** _Decided: a tag from the setting `shopify_gift_tags` on an order whose total is 0 (recommended)._ Other options: a list of 100 percent discount codes; every order with a total of 0. Why: a tag is explicit and cheap to change, while a zero total alone says nothing about why an order is free; how the pilot brand enters gifts and whether influencers pay shipping is asked below (brand question 3), and the setting can follow the answer without a code change.
4. **Q-13.4 With two stores selling from one warehouse, how is stock shared?** _Decided: both stores receive the full sellable quantity, and an order in one store pushes the other within seconds (recommended)._ Other options: a fixed share per store; a variant sold online in one store only. Why: the reference brand's second store sells the first store's SKUs by design, so a split would hide stock from a store that sells it; pushing on intake cuts the window in which the last piece sells twice from about a minute to seconds.
5. **Q-13.5 What happens to lines added to a Shopify order after it was fulfilled (an exchange made by editing the delivered order)?** _Decided: they become a linked replacement order through `replacement_for`, reserved and visible to the warehouse (recommended)._ Other options: keep "edit not applied" with one `exchange_in_shopify` attention and never push Shopify's lowered quantity back up; ask the pilot brand to make replacements as new Shopify orders until slice 1b. Why: the reference brand exchanges by editing the delivered order, and without a replacement order the piece is never held and the push could sell it again.
6. **Q-13.6 Which Shopify side stock differences does the push overwrite, and when?** _Decided: a Shopify figure higher than the ledger is pushed down the next minute; a lower one waits for a person or for the ledger to fall to it (recommended)._ Other options: every difference pushed the next minute; every difference waits for the nightly reconcile and is then overwritten. Why: higher is an oversell risk that cannot wait; lower is usually deliberate (a damaged piece, an exchange line) and is the safe side.
7. **Q-13.7 How do orders that are already open at go live arrive?** _Decided: orders placed before the go live instant enter as `confirmed`, later ones as `new` (recommended)._ Other options: all as `new` with a "before go live" marker the queue hides, confirmed in bulk; all as `new`. Why: the team confirmed those orders on WhatsApp before the app existed, and calling 120 customers again on day one would wreck the first week's figures.
8. **Q-13.8 Who connects the stores?** _Decided: the vendor's onboarding person on a call with the owner, with the owner typing the secret into the app (recommended)._ Other options: the owner alone from the runbook; the owner sends the keys to the vendor. Why: the setup has many steps whose failures look alike, and sending keys over WhatsApp puts a store's secret in a chat history; the runbook rules that out.

**Ask the pilot brand** (facts only the brand knows, from the [domain review](../../reviews/domain-shopify-connector.md); each answer may change a setting or a runbook step, not the design):
1. Who owns your Shopify stores' organization: you, or the agency or freelancer who built them? Can you open the Dev Dashboard for each store yourself? (Q-13.1, Q-13.8)
2. When does the Bosta Shopify app create the delivery, and does it mark the Shopify order fulfilled at that moment? (AC-13)
3. How do you enter an influencer gift today: a draft order at 100 percent off, a discount code, or a tag? Does the influencer pay the shipping? And how is an exchange replacement entered: an edit of the delivered order, or a new order? (Q-13.3, Q-13.5)
4. Which payment gateway names appear on your orders? Does your COD form app use its own name? Do you record InstaPay or wallet deposits in Shopify, and how? (AC-17)
5. Do the same SKUs sell in both stores, and do launches go live on both at once? (Q-13.4)
6. How many locations does each Shopify store have, and does any of them other than the warehouse hold stock? (AC-22)
7. How are influencer discount codes named, and can an order carry two codes? (`discountCode`)
8. Who changes stock in the Shopify admin today, and why: damaged pieces, held pieces, launches? (Q-13.6)
9. Do you sell through Shopify POS or create draft orders for replacements? (Follow-up)
